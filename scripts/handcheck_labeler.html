      §6 taxonomy — the rule the judge was given
      
REAL — the work exists and the attribution identifies that work. Includes a real work with metadata errors that do not change which work is meant: garbled or misspelled author names, author-order swaps within the real coauthor list, title or venue typos, missing middle initials, off-by-one pages, a non-resolving DOI where title + venue + year still pick out the real work.

      
FABRICATED — positive evidence it does not exist or is a different work. Either (a) no work matches the cited title / case name / DOI / venue anywhere, or (b) the identifier resolves to a different real work than the cited byline implies.

      
UNVERIFIABLE — you genuinely cannot determine it either way after reasonable effort.

      
The dividing line: does the rest of the attribution still uniquely identify the real work the title points at? A garbled author name on an otherwise-correct title/year/venue is REAL. A byline that itself names a different real paper is FABRICATED. Default a real-work-with-metadata-error to REAL.

      
Two places you should differ from the judge: search as much as you need (it had a 3-search budget, you are the gold standard), and therefore use UNVERIFIABLE sparingly — yours should mean genuinely undeterminable, not out of budget.
