12. **Verifier-REAL trust step fails on the Phase C corpus; corrective re-judge of at-risk citations (2026-08-11, blind hand-check of the Phase C confirmatory corpus).**

    - *What changed:* Amendment #6(c) restricted the J2a judge to fire only on verifier-`FABRICATED` and verifier-`UNVERIFIABLE` citations, accepting verifier-`REAL` at face value. That restriction was justified by Step 0 evidence putting verifier-`REAL` precision at 20/21 ≈ 95%, and was validated at production scale on **Phase B** by the registered EXISTENT_WRONG_ID sub-study (population-reweighted disagreement 4.3%, design-based 95% CI [1.7%, 7.0%], clearing the pre-committed 8% gate). **On the Phase C open-weights corpus that trust step does not hold: a blind hand-check found 8 of 20 verifier-`REAL` citations were fabricated, 40.0% (Wilson 95% CI [21.9%, 61.3%]).** Even the interval's lower bound is roughly three times the registered gate. This amendment therefore (a) records the failure, and (b) applies a corrective re-judge in which the LLM judge additionally fires on verifier-`REAL` citations, restricted to those that can still change a generation-level outcome. Verdicts are written to a separate file (`data/judgments/phase_c/verifier_real_rejudge.jsonl`) and applied by an explicit opt-in flag (`--verifier-real-rejudgments`), so the registered amendment #6(c) pipeline remains the default and the deviation is auditable and reversible.

    - *Evidence:* A fabrication-enriched blind hand-check of the Phase C corpus, 150 citations, stratified over four strata with design weights (`src/analysis/handcheck_sample.py`, seed 20260811; frozen sample and labels at `data/handcheck/phase_c/`). Blinding: the annotator's file carried no verifier, judge, combined, stratum, weight or era field, presentation order was shuffled across strata, and the label strings appear zero times in the file. **Two deliberate departures from the Step 0 hand-check, both tightening it:** no pipeline evidence was pre-filled (Step 0 supplied the verifier's own lookups, which risks measuring anchoring, since the verifier is part of what is under validation), and the sample is enriched rather than representative. **The Step 0 96.0% and this study's numbers are therefore not comparable and are not presented as a trend.** Results, unweighted and conditional per stratum: verifier-`REAL` trusted **12/20 = 60.0%**; judge-rescued-to-`REAL` **43/45 = 95.6%** [85.2, 98.8]; judge-`FABRICATED` **48/60 = 80.0%** [68.2, 88.2]; judge-`UNVERIFIABLE` 6/13 = 46.2%. Design-weighted population accuracy **0.719** against a trivial always-`REAL` baseline of **0.546**, exact McNemar **p = 5.6e-05**; `FABRICATED` recall **0.781** [0.673, 0.860] on 73 human-labelled fabrications. Full result at `data/analysis/phase_c/handcheck_result.json`.

    - *Why the correction is applied rather than disclosed:* The registered outcome is generation-level, so a missed fabrication changes a result only when it is the sole bad citation in its generation. Of 25,530 citing generations, 12,920 are already scored `fabricated = 1` and cannot flip, and only **5,251 are at risk** (scored 0 and holding at least one verifier-`REAL` citation). Expected flips at the hand-check point estimate were **2,442 generations, 9.6% of citing generations** (bounds 5.5% to 13.9%), so the absolute fabrication rate was understated by roughly ten points, not forty. **Realized: 1,458 generations flipped, 5.71%, at the bottom edge of that interval.** The projection used the 40.0% hand-check rate on 20 citations; the corrective run judged 26.8% of the 6,852 at-risk citations FABRICATED, so the smaller realized shift is the wide hand-check interval resolving low, not a failure of the correction. **The decisive problem is not the level but the differential exposure.** At-risk share and mean trusted-citation load by condition: `citations_expected` 26.4% / 1.64, `abstention_permitted` 23.5% / 1.18, `neutral` 18.9% / 1.10, `length_matched` 14.6% / 1.09. Under an independence approximation the correction adds ≈ +15.0pp to `citations_expected` and +6.2pp to `length_matched`, a differential of ≈ 8.8pp. **Amendment #10 pins Δ = `citations_expected` − `length_matched` with bin boundaries at ±5pp and registers a negative Δ as a loss for the citation-specific claim. A differential bias larger than the bin width could manufacture that verdict on its own.** Disclosure alone would leave the paper's most consequential pre-committed directional call resting on an artifact. Exposure by `stage_ordinal` is by contrast nearly flat (19.7% to 22.7%), so **E1 is not materially implicated**, consistent with the null `stage_ordinal × era` drift interaction.

    - *Scope of the corrective run:* Only verifier-`REAL` citations inside at-risk generations are judged: **6,852 of 8,860 (77.3%)**, sitting in 5,251 at-risk generations. Generations already scored fabricated cannot change and generations holding no verifier-`REAL` citation have no exposure, so this makes every generation-level outcome exact rather than estimated, at roughly 14 nights of Max-pool dispatch instead of 18. Prompts are produced by the registered renderer (`build_judge_inputs` with `fire_on_statuses=("REAL",)`), so the instrument is byte-identical to the deployed judge and only the population differs. Model pinned `model="sonnet"` per amendment #6(d); tool stack Claude Code `WebSearch` + `WebFetch`, the same disclosed divergence as amendment #6(d). Implementation and tests: `src/analysis/verifier_real_rejudge.py`, `tests/test_verifier_real_rejudge.py`; application step `src/analysis/build_confirmatory_inputs.py::apply_verifier_real_rejudgments`.

    - *Result of the corrective run (completed 2026-08-30):* All **6,852** at-risk citations were re-judged, with 6,852 unique ids, no duplicates and no error rows; the persisted ids are exactly the registered population. Verdicts on citations the structured verifier had called `REAL`: **`REAL` 4,828 (70.5%), `FABRICATED` 1,836 (26.8%), `UNVERIFIABLE` 188 (2.7%)**. So **29.5% of verifier-`REAL` labels did not survive re-judging**, against the 40.0% [21.9, 61.3] hand-check estimate that triggered this amendment, which is inside that interval. **This 29.5% is a net figure over two opposing corrections and is reported as a misclassification matrix, never as a scalar:** demotions where a wrong identifier resolves to a different real work, against rescues where a wrong venue, wrong initials or a dead DOI still leave one real work uniquely identified. The applied dividing line is the one in §6(b): a wrong identifier is a fabrication **only if it resolves to a different real work**, and a dead DOI accompanied by a title, author and year that identify one real work is `REAL`. The 188 `UNVERIFIABLE` re-judgments leave the verifier's `REAL` label standing rather than becoming exclusions, matching the main combine rule, so **6,664 of the 6,852 are applied** and no generation moves into or out of the NA-excluded set. Judge grounding held throughout the corrective run, zero-search 0.72% and mean search count 1.571, with none of the era structure seen in the main judge stage. At the generation level the correction moves **1,458 of 25,530 citing generations (5.71%)**, every one of them from clean to `fabricated`, taking the absolute fabrication rate from 50.61% to 56.32%. Artifact `data/judgments/phase_c/verifier_real_rejudge.jsonl`; impact counts reproducible with `scripts/_rejudge_generation_impact.py`.

    - *Residual limitation, stated rather than claimed away:* This replaces blind verifier trust with the judge's own verdict, and the same hand-check puts the judge at **80.0% precision [68.2, 88.2]** on `FABRICATED`. The corrected outcome therefore carries roughly 20% residual error on that stratum rather than none. The correction is a substantial improvement on 40% blind trust, **not an elimination of error**, and the paper reports it that way.

    - *Effect on the registered analysis:* No per-cell, per-test, or estimand change is induced for the §6 confirmatory hypothesis family or the §13 GLMM specifications. The outcome coding (per-generation binary `fabricated`, UNVERIFIABLE-only NA-excluded) is unchanged. What changes is the accuracy of the citation-level labels feeding that outcome. **Both analyses are reported side by side: the registered amendment #6(c) pipeline (correction withheld) and the corrected pipeline, with the amendment #10 Δ reported under both.** If the two agree, the trust-step failure is shown not to reach the registered conclusions; if they disagree, the corrected analysis is the primary and the divergence is the finding. Citation-level and generation-level counts are in the result bullet above: 6,852 re-judged, 1,836 flipped to `FABRICATED`, 1,458 generations flipped.

**Δ under both pipelines (computed 2026-09-04, after the one-shot confirmatory fit, deliberately not before it, because a per-condition flip count IS the amendment #10 Δ): the two pipelines AGREE on the registered bin.** Corrected **Δ = +28.35pp** [+25.58, +31.12]; registered **Δ = +12.64pp** [+10.75, +14.53]. Both sit in the Δ ≥ +5pp bin, and neither confidence interval comes near the ±5pp boundary, so the boundary-ambiguity rule does not engage in either. The registered bin is therefore **"the citation cue, not mere verbosity, drives the effect"** under the corrected and the withheld-correction analyses alike. **This is the outcome this amendment set as the test: the two agree, so the verifier-REAL trust-step failure does not reach the registered conclusion.**

The magnitude gap between the pipelines, 15.7pp, is in the direction and for the reason predicted above: exposure to the trust failure is highest in `citations_expected` (26.4% of generations at risk, mean 1.64 trusted citations) and lowest in `length_matched` (14.6%, 1.09), so the uncorrected pipeline understates precisely this contrast. The projection made before the corrective run estimated the differential at ≈ 8.8pp under an independence approximation; the realized differential is larger. **What matters for the registered conclusion is the direction of the residual risk: the withheld-correction pipeline is the conservative one, and it still clears the boundary by more than 5pp of margin, so the finding does not rest on the correction having been applied.**

Also worth recording because it inverts the premise of amendment #10: the Phase B prior that motivated the negative-delta bin put Δ at ≈ −2.0pp with `length_matched` ABOVE `citations_expected`. Phase C reverses that ordering decisively under both pipelines (odds ratios versus neutral: corrected 5.40 vs 1.60, registered 2.88 vs 1.73). Amendment #10's own honesty note anticipated exactly this, that a single small closed-API model's length-compliance behavior might not transfer to open-weights checkpoints. The negative-delta rule was fixed in advance and did not fire.

    - *Why Phase C differs from Phase B is not established.* The Phase B sub-study and this hand-check measure the same quantity on different corpora: Phase B on Claude Haiku 4.5 generations, Phase C on twelve open-weights checkpoints. Open-weights models may produce a different mix of failure modes, including citations carrying real DOIs attached to different real works, which the structured verifier resolves as `REAL`. This is a hypothesis, not a finding, and no causal claim is made.
